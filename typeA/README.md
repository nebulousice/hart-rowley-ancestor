## Summary

A Coxeter group is a group generated by finitely many elements of order two, subject only to relations fixing the order of each product of two generators; the number of generators is the *rank*. The *length* of an element is the least number of generators whose product it is. A *prefix* of $`w`$ is an element $`u`$ such that some shortest word for $`w`$ begins with a shortest word for $`u`$. S. B. Hart and P. J. Rowley conjectured that, in every finite Coxeter group, each element $`w\ne e`$ has exactly one longest prefix that is an involution, the *ancestor* $`a(w)`$ (their Conjecture 1.2, since proved for all Coxeter groups by C. Gaetz and Y. Gao). Writing $`w=a(w)\cdot w'`$ and repeating on $`w'`$ splits $`w`$ greedily into involutions; the number of factors is the *involution length* $`\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)`$. Their Conjecture 1.3 says that the involution length is at most the rank. Salem Levinstein showed that this is false in general (counterexamples in $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$, $`E_6`$, and hence $`E_7`$). This paper proves that it holds in type A, that is, for the symmetric groups, and that the bound is attained.

## The theorems

Let $`S_N`$ be the group of permutations of $`\{1,\dots,N\}`$, generated by the adjacent transpositions $`s_i=(i\ \ i+1)`$. It is the Coxeter group of type $`A_{N-1}`$, of rank $`N-1`$. The *displacement* of a permutation $`w`$ is its largest shift:

```math
\mathrm{disp}(w)=\max_{1\le i\le N}|w(i)-i|.
```

**Theorem A.** For every $`N\ge 1`$ and every $`w\in S_N`$,

```math
\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)\le\mathrm{disp}(w)\le N-1=\mathrm{rank}(S_N).
```

So Conjecture 1.3 holds in type A. Both inequalities are equalities for the Coxeter element $`s_1s_2\cdots s_{N-1}`$. The key step shows that removing the ancestor strictly lowers the displacement. It uses the theorem of Gaetz and Gao, whose proof the paper writes out in full from textbook facts, so the paper does not rely on their unrefereed preprint as an authority.

The group $`B_n`$ of *signed permutations* consists of the permutations $`w`$ of $`\{\pm1,\dots,\pm n\}`$ with $`w(-x)=-w(x)`$. Relabelling $`\{\pm1,\dots,\pm n\}`$ in order as $`\{1,\dots,2n\}`$ makes $`B_n`$ a subgroup of $`S_{2n}`$.

**Theorem B.** For every $`n\ge 1`$ and every $`w\in B_n`$, the involution length of $`w`$ in $`B_n`$ equals its involution length in $`S_{2n}`$. Hence

```math
\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}^{B}(w)\le 2n-1.
```

Theorem B is **not** Conjecture 1.3 for type B: the rank of $`B_n`$ is $`n`$, and $`2n-1>n`$ for $`n\ge 2`$. Conjecture 1.3 for $`B_n`$ remains open here. The same reduction is false for type D already in $`D_3`$: the element with window $`(-3,-1,2)`$ has involution length 2 in $`D_3`$ and 3 in $`S_6`$.

The paper's Appendix A is their argument, written out in our notation.

Hart and Rowley reported a computer check of Conjectures 1.2 and 1.3 for the irreducible finite Coxeter groups of order below 100,000 (their §3); Levinstein notes that this check is incorrect for Conjecture 1.3 in $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$ and $`E_6`$.
