## The model

`Main.lean` works in $G$, the group of permutations of the 30 roots of $H_3$ generated by three explicit permutations `s1`, `s2`, `s3`. The Coxeter graph is $r_1 \overset{5}{-} r_2 - r_3$. Elements are indices into the list `elemsData`. Multiplication is composition of permutations.

The definitions follow the source literally:
- `len` is the word length, and `lenData_ok` proves it equals the breadth-first distance from the identity in the Cayley graph;
- an involution `u` is a prefix of `w` when `len u + len (u w) = len w` (`isPrefixInv`). Since $u^{-1}=u$, this is the criterion $\ell(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w)=\ell(w)$, which is equivalent to the source's definition (Lemma 1 of [`PROOF.md`](PROOF.md));
- `ancestors w` is the list of involution prefixes of maximal length;
- `decomp` peels the ancestor on the left and recurses on the remainder. It returns `none` if some ancestor is not unique. `ilen` is the number of factors.

## What the Lean statement does not cover

The Lean theorems are statements about the concrete group $G$ and the Lean definitions. Four short arguments connect them to the mathematics. None of them is stated in Lean.

1. **$G$ is the Coxeter group $H_3$.** By `relations_H3`, $r_i\mapsto s_i$ defines a homomorphism from $W(H_3)$ onto $G$. By `table_ok` and `bfs_reaches_all`, $\lvert G\rvert=120$. The cited fact $\lvert W(H_3)\rvert=120$ makes the homomorphism an isomorphism. So word length in $G$ is the Coxeter length.
2. **Products stay in the list.** `isPrefixInv` looks up the product $uw$ with `idx`, which would return the out-of-range value 120 if the product were missing. It never is: the 120 listed elements contain the identity and are closed under right multiplication by the generators (`table_ok`), so they form the whole group generated by `s1`, `s2`, `s3`, which is closed under products.
3. **The step bound of `decomp` is never reached.** `decomp` takes a step bound of 20 and returns an empty tail if the bound runs out. Each step removes an ancestor $a$ with `len a + len (a w) = len w` and `len a` at least 1, so the length drops by at least 1. The maximum length is 15 (`lenData_ok`). So at most 15 steps occur, fewer than 20.
4. **The source's hypothesis.** Conjecture 1.3 assumes Conjecture 1.2. `h3_table` checks the ancestor property for all of $H_3$ directly, so the $H_3$ counterexample does not depend on the theorem of Gaetz and Gao.

**Comparison with Levinstein's Lean proof.** Levinstein's report ([s66lem/conjectures PR #15](https://github.com/s66lem/conjectures/pull/15)) states its $H_3$ theorem for every abstract `CoxeterSystem CoxeterMatrix.H₃ W` of Mathlib, as recorded by our literature check (we did not re-check that build). That statement is stronger than ours: it does not rely on a cited group order.

## What would make the formalization stronger

- Prove $\lvert W(H_3)\rvert=120$ in Lean, or build the isomorphism with Mathlib's `CoxeterMatrix.H₃` (through `CoxeterSystem.lift` and a proof of injectivity). Note that Mathlib labels $H_3$ differently: the edge labelled 5 joins its second and third generators.
- State the step-bound and closure arguments (items 2 and 3 above) as Lean lemmas.
- The same technique could be tried on $F_4$ (1152 elements); $H_4$ (14400) and $E_6$ (51840) are much larger. Not attempted.
