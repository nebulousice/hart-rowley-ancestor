# Hart–Rowley Conjecture 1.3: counterexamples in $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$, $`H_4`$ and $`E_6`$, and a proof in type $`A`$

*Write-up of 2026-11-27. **Expert verification required.** Verified so far with: Lean 4 (the H3 counterexample); two independent exact computer certificates; AI review of the type A proof (Claude Opus checker, Claude Fable adversary, Claude Sonnet arbiter; round 2: all three accept with zero issues). The type A theorem is not formalized in Lean.*

> **In one line:** Hart and Rowley conjectured that every element of a finite Coxeter group splits greedily into at most "rank" many involutions. S. Levinstein refuted this first, in public. This repository checks the refutation independently: a hand proof and a Lean proof for $`H_3`$, and exact maxima computed by two independent programs for $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$, $`H_4`$ and $`E_6`$. It also proves that the conjecture does hold in type $`A`$, that is, for the symmetric groups.

## Summary

**Theorem.** In the Coxeter group $`H_3`$, with Coxeter graph $`r_1 \overset{5}{-} r_2 - r_3`$, the element

```math
w=r_2r_1r_2r_3r_1r_2r_1
```

has length 7 and ancestor decomposition

```math
w=(r_2r_1r_2)(r_1r_3)(r_2)(r_1),
```

so its involution length is $4$, larger than the rank $3$. More generally, the maximum involution length $M(W)$ is

```math
M(H_3)=4,\qquad M(F_4)=5,\qquad M(H_4)=7,\qquad M(E_6)=8,
```

larger than the ranks $3$, $4$, $4$, $6$. So Conjecture 1.3 of S. B. Hart and P. J. Rowley, *A note on involution prefixes in Coxeter groups*, [arXiv:2502.00777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00777) (Arch. Math. 126 (2026) 231–237, [doi:10.1007/s00013-025-02220-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-025-02220-z)), is false. Numerical computations also give $`M(E_7)=9`$ and $`M(E_8)\ge 9`$.

**Priority.** This is **not the first refutation.**
- **Salem Levinstein refuted Conjecture 1.3 first**, in public, on 2026-11-27: Zenodo preprint [10.5281/zenodo.24581942](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24581942) (created 08:30 UTC) and [s66lem/conjectures PR #15](https://github.com/s66lem/conjectures/pull/15) (merged 08:32 UTC). That report gives counterexamples in $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$ and $`E_6`$, the maxima $`M(H_3)=4`$, $`M(F_4)=5`$, $`M(E_6)=8`$, the bound $`M(E_7)\ge 8`$, and a Lean 4 proof for $`H_3`$.
- Our computations were made **independently**, on the same day, in a private repository, without knowledge of Levinstein's report. They confirm Levinstein's values for $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$ and $`E_6`$ with two independent exact programs. [`LITERATURE_CHECK.md`](LITERATURE_CHECK.md) gives the timeline and a side-by-side comparison.
- Three values here are not in Levinstein's report: $`M(H_4)=7`$ (computer-verified), $`M(E_7)=9`$ and $`M(E_8)\ge 9`$ (both numerical only). Our literature search of 2026-11-27 did not find them in any other source either. This is a search result, not a priority claim.
- Conjecture 1.2 (uniqueness of the ancestor) was proved for every Coxeter group by C. Gaetz and Y. Gao, *Separable elements and Bruhat orders*, [arXiv:2610.22857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22857) (2026-10-16). So the hypothesis of Conjecture 1.3 holds. Our counterexamples do not rely on their theorem: we check uniqueness directly.
- The conjectures are Hart and Rowley's. They reported a Magma verification that includes $`H_3`$, $`H_4`$, $`F_4`$ and $`E_6`$; our computations contradict that report for these four groups.

## The conjecture holds in type $`A`$

Let $`S_N`$ be the symmetric group on $`\{1,\dots,N\}`$. With the adjacent transpositions $`s_i=(i\ \ i+1)`$ as generators, it is the Coxeter group of type $`A_{N-1}`$, of rank $`N-1`$. The **displacement** of a permutation $w$ is the largest distance by which it moves a point:

```math
\mathrm{disp}(w)=\max_{1\le i\le N}\lvert w(i)-i\rvert .
```

The involution length $`\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}`$ is the number of factors in the greedy decomposition (defined under *Setup* below).

**Theorem (type $`A`$).** For every $`N\ge1`$ and every $`w\in S_N`$,

```math
\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)\le\mathrm{disp}(w)\le N-1 .
```

So Hart–Rowley Conjecture 1.3 holds for the Coxeter groups of type $A$. Both inequalities are equalities for $`w=s_1s_2\cdots s_{N-1}`$.

**Idea of the proof.** The proof shows that removing the first factor (the ancestor) strictly lowers the displacement. Since the displacement is at most $`N-1`$ and drops by at least one with each factor, there are at most that many factors. Two steps give the drop.
- The remainder lies below $w$ in the Bruhat order, and displacement cannot grow in that order. (The **Bruhat order** is the partial order generated by $`x<xt`$ for every transposition $t$ with $`\ell(xt)>\ell(x)`$.)
- For a point that $w$ moves by the full displacement, an explicit involution prefix of $w$ is built that moves it. A comparison in the Bruhat order carries this over to the remainder, which therefore cannot move that point as far.

**What the proof uses.** The second step needs one deep fact: the ancestor is unique, and every involution prefix lies below it in the Bruhat order. This is Corollary 1.2 of C. Gaetz and Y. Gao, *Separable elements and Bruhat orders*, [arXiv:2610.22857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22857), an unrefereed preprint. Our proof does not take it on authority. An appendix proves it in full from textbook facts about Coxeter groups (the lifting property and the subword property of the Bruhat order; A. Björner and F. Brenti, *Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups*, Springer, 2005, [doi:10.1007/3-540-27596-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-27596-7)). The argument of that appendix is Gaetz and Gao's, and is credited to them.

**A consequence in type $`B`$.** The group $`B_n`$ of signed permutations sits inside $`S_{2n}`$. Its prefix order (the weak order) is the restriction of the prefix order of $`S_{2n}`$. So ancestors and involution lengths in $`B_n`$ agree with those computed in $`S_{2n}`$, and the theorem above gives

```math
\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)\le 2n-1\qquad\text{for every } w\in B_n .
```

This is **not** Hart–Rowley Conjecture 1.3 for type $B$. The rank of $`B_n`$ is $n$, and $`2n-1>n`$ for every $`n\ge2`$. The conjecture for type $B$ is still open.

The same reduction fails in type $D$, already in $`D_3`$. For the signed permutation with window $`(w(1),w(2),w(3))=(-3,-1,2)`$, the involution length is 2 in $`D_3`$ but 3 in $`S_6`$. In $`D_3`$, 6 ancestors and 4 involution lengths differ from the values in $`S_6`$; in $`D_4`$, 55 and 33.

**Status of these two results.** Both are **proved**: the written proof passed two rounds of AI review, ending with all three referees accepting with zero issues ([`REVIEWS.md`](REVIEWS.md)). Exact computer checks over all of $`S_N`$ for $`N\le7`$, $`B_n`$ for $`n\le4`$, and $`D_3`$, $`D_4`$ agree with both statements; they are corroboration only, and no step of the proof uses them. Neither result is formalized in Lean. The full written proof is not yet included in this folder.

## How the counterexamples were checked

**Method.**
1. Take the definitions literally from the source, and check them against the source's own worked example in $`A_6`$. That example tells the left-hand version from the right-hand one, so the reading is fixed.
2. Prove the $`H_3`$ case by hand. Matsumoto's and Tits's theorems on reduced words give all reduced words of $w$ and of its prefixes. A prefix is an involution exactly when the reverse of a reduced word is again a reduced word. Four rounds of this give the decomposition.
3. Enumerate $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$ and $`H_4`$ completely with two independently written exact programs (root permutations over $`\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]`$; Tits matrices over $`\mathbb{Z}[\varphi]`$ or $`\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt2]`$). They agree on every value. For $`E_6`$, the first program and a separate exploratory program agree.
4. Check the $`H_3`$ case in Lean 4 by kernel evaluation, in a concrete permutation model of order 120.
5. Explore $`E_7`$ (2,903,040 elements) with one fast program, re-check the witness with a single-element checker, and pass it to $`E_8`$ through the parabolic subgroup $`E_7\subset E_8`$.

**Side facts.**
- Three other greedy readings (peeling on the right, or alternating sides) also exceed the rank: their maxima are 4 in $`H_3`$ and 5 in $`F_4`$. So the failure does not come from the choice of side.
- A *heuristic* explanation of the source's Magma claim: the minimal number of involution factors in a length-additive factorization (a non-greedy count) does satisfy the rank bound in $`H_3`$, $`F_4`$, $`H_4`$ and $`E_6`$. A computation of that count would have reported the conjecture as verified there. This is a guess about code we have not seen.

## Research log

### Setup

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite Coxeter system: a group $W$ generated by a set $S$ of involutions subject only to relations $`(st)^{m(s,t)}=e`$. The **rank** is the number of generators. The **length** $`\ell(w)`$ is the length of a shortest word for $w$ in the generators; such a word is **reduced**.

Hart and Rowley define:
- $u$ is a **prefix** of $w$ if some reduced word of $w$ begins with a reduced word of $u$ (equivalently, $`\ell(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w)=\ell(w)`$);
- an **involution** is an element $`u\ne e`$ with $u^2=e$;
- the **ancestor** $a(w)$ is the longest involution prefix of $w$, when it is unique (the ancestor property);
- the **ancestor decomposition** peels off $a(w)$ on the left and repeats on the remainder $`a(w)^{-1}w`$;
- the **involution length** $`\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)`$ is the number of factors, with $`\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(e)=0`$.

Conjecture 1.2 says the ancestor is always unique in a finite Coxeter group. Conjecture 1.3 says $`\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)`$ is at most the rank.

### Notation

Coxeter graphs used:

```math
H_3:\ r_1 \overset{5}{-} r_2 - r_3,\qquad H_4:\ r_1 \overset{5}{-} r_2 - r_3 - r_4,\qquad F_4:\ r_1 - r_2 \overset{4}{-} r_3 - r_4 .
```

$`E_n`$ ($n=6,7,8$) uses Bourbaki's labelling: the path $`r_1 - r_3 - r_4 - \cdots - r_n`$, with $`r_2`$ joined to $`r_4`$.

```math
M(W)=\max_{w\in W}\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w),\qquad \mathrm{disp}(w)=\max_i\lvert w(i)-i\rvert\ \ (w\in S_N).
```

$`S_N`$ is the symmetric group, the Coxeter group of type $`A_{N-1}`$ (rank $N-1$). A factorization $`w=u_1\cdots u_k`$ is **length-additive** if the lengths add up. $m(w)$ is the least number of involutions in a length-additive factorization of $w$; since the ancestor decomposition is one, $`m(w)\le\ell_{\mathrm{inv}}(w)`$.
